## TOPIC A — AI for turn-based strategy / tactics

### A.1 Architecture catalogue

#### Behaviour trees (BT)

- **Evidence.** A peer-reviewed comparison of BT vs HTN planning *for player experience impact* exists: [Comparing Behaviour Tree and Hierarchical Task Network Planning Methods for their Impact on Player Experience](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/abstract/document/10371841) **[T]**. A canonical theoretical treatment is the arXiv monograph [Behavior Trees in Robotics and AI: An Introduction](http://xxx.itp.ac.cn/abs/1709.00084v6) **[T]** — note this is a mirror host of arXiv:1709.00084; the body was not read.
- **Evidence (weak).** [Architecture Selection Reference](https://github.com/erikhazzard/vasir/blob/main/.agents/skills/game-ai__architecting-ai/references/architecture_selection.md) **[T-weak]** is an in-repo "skill" reference document that appears to compare candidate game-AI architectures. It is a secondary, machine-authored artefact — cite it only as a pointer, never as authority.
- **[I]** BTs excel at *reactivity with authored structure* and have predictable per-tick cost proportional to tree depth, which suits a handful to a few dozen units whose behaviour must be legible to designers. They do **not** by themselves answer "which of these 12 possible actions is best right now" — that is a scoring problem (utility) or a search problem (planner/MCTS).
- **[U]** No benchmark comparing BT tick cost against utility scoring at hundreds of agents was found.

- **Evidence (fragment).** *"Utility systems can be extended with several mechanisms to improve the behaviors that they produce"* — [ProjectReport.pdf (University of Western Ontario)](https://www.csd.uwo.ca/~ebuitron/downloads/ProjectReport.pdf) **[F]**. This is the only verbatim statement about utility-AI extensibility I obtained.
- **Evidence (implementation/performance concern).** A utility-AI library ships a dedicated performance document: [Trove `com.trove.utilityai` — performance.md](https://github.com/PhilSA/Trove/blob/saved/vo-old/com.trove.utilityai/Documentation%7E/performance.md) **[T]**. The existence of a performance chapter for a utility-AI package is itself evidence that **utility scoring cost is a known engineering concern at scale**.
- **Evidence (questions practitioners ask).** [Structuring Actions in Utility AI](https://gamedev.stackexchange.com/questions/214432/structuring-actions-in-utility-ai) **[T-weak]** — a Stack Exchange question on how to structure actions, i.e. the authoring/modeling problem.
- **[I]** Utility AI degrades gracefully with unit count: cost is O(units × considerations) per decision tick, it is embarrassingly parallel, and each unit's decision is independent — which is exactly why it is the natural default for hundreds of units in a TBS, *provided* decisions are time-sliced and considerations are cheap to evaluate. Its weakness is emergent incoherence (many locally-good choices can produce a globally bad turn) and the difficulty of authoring/debugging response curves.
- **[U]** No source found measuring utility-AI cost per unit per turn at 100/500/1000 units.

#### HTN (Hierarchical Task Network) planning

- **Evidence.** A peer-reviewed BT-vs-HTN comparison (player-experience lens) exists: [Comparing Behaviour Tree and Hierarchical Task Network Planning Methods…](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/abstract/document/10371841) **[T]**. A planning-implementation paper notes that **[F]** *"In JSHOP2 (Ilghami and Nau, 2003), methods are written in the same Lisp-like language, but JSHOP2 compiles them to Java to perfo[rm]…"* — [nau2021gtpyhop.pdf (Univ. of Maryland)](http://www.cs.umd.edu/~nau/papers/nau2021gtpyhop.pdf) **[F]**. This fragment is directly relevant to the *performance* question: **an HTN planner implementation chose compilation as its performance strategy.**
- **[I]** HTNs sit between BTs and full search: the designer writes decomposition methods, so plan quality is bounded by authored knowledge and planning is far cheaper and more predictable than generative planning. For TBS scale, HTN's appeal is that a "squad plan" can decompose hierarchically into "unit orders", which maps naturally onto the few-units-vs-hundreds-of-units axis.
- **[U]** No cost model, benchmark, or production postmortem for HTN in a shipped turn-based tactics game was found.

#### MCTS (Monte Carlo Tree Search)

- **Evidence (fragment).** A dissertation states: **[F]** *"The purpose behind using MCTS in this game comes from its ability to handle the large search space, to allow for fine-grained co[ntrol]…"* — [dissertação (Técnico Lisboa)](https://fenix.tecnico.ulisboa.pt/downloadFile/1689244997263072/94042-Manuel-Roxo_dissertacao.pdf) **[F]**. This is the clearest fragment on *why* MCTS is chosen: **large search spaces and fine-grained control.**
- **Evidence.** TBS-specific search research exists: [Evolutionary Tree Search for Turn-Based Strategy Games](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1007/978-3-032-23657-9_12) **[T]** (published in *Advances in Computer Games*, per the title), and [Optimizing Monte Carlo Tree Search for General Game Playing](http://www.jfdc.cnic.cn/EN/abstract/abstract226.shtml) **[T]**.
- **Evidence (branching-factor reduction).** A Japanese-language paper title translates to *"Effect of grouping nodes in turn-based strategy games"* — [jstage](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/fss/35/0/35_382/_article/-char/en) **[T]**. **[I]** "Grouping nodes" is a search-space-reduction technique; the title implies TBS branching factor is the core obstacle. *I could not read the paper to confirm the technique.*
- **Evidence (scale).** [GAL: Towards Large Simulations with Tens of Agents](https://rd.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/11550617_45) **[T]** — a title that directly names the "tens of agents" scale.
- **[I]** MCTS is attractive but *turn-hungry*: its cost is a budget you choose, and a TBS turn with hundreds of units cannot afford a per-unit MCTS unless the move space is factored (per-unit sequential search) or abstracted (grouped nodes, as above). MCTS also presupposes a cheap forward simulator and a well-shaped evaluation of leaf states — for a tactics game that means the entire combat/damage/LOS model must be runnable headless, fast, and deterministically.
- **[U]** No source found quantifying MCTS feasibility at 100+ units in a shipped TBS.

#### Minimax / alpha-beta

- **Evidence.** A study comparing minimax approximation and alpha-beta pruning in exactly our genre: [PERBANDINGAN ALGORITMA MINIMAX APPROXIMATION DAN ALPHA-BETA PRUNING PADA ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE DALAM TURN BASED TACTICAL ROLE-PLAYING GAME](https://repository.itera.ac.id/depan/submission/SB2601120050) **[T]** — *"Comparison of Minimax Approximation and Alpha-Beta Pruning Algorithms for AI in a Turn-Based Tactical Role-Playing Game."*
- **Evidence (fragment).** **[F]** *"If it is the minimizer's turn and we find a move with a better value than beta (smaller than beta), we update the beta para[meter]"* — [Charles University thesis](https://dspace.cuni.cz/bitstream/handle/20.500.11956/193101/130401921.pdf) **[F]**. Textbook alpha-beta mechanics; confirms only that the standard formulation is what's being described, not any TBS-specific result.
- **[I]** Alpha-beta is viable for a *tactical* TBS only where the move space per turn is small and depth is shallow (chess-like). With hundreds of units and a combined-arms move space, full-width minimax explodes; the practical variants are local search around a subset of units, "one unit at a time" sequential search, and abstraction. This is consistent with the node-grouping paper above **[T]**.
- **[U]** No source giving a concrete branching-factor budget for a squad/tactics TBS.

#### Influence maps

- **Evidence.** Dave Mark authored [Modular Tactical Influence Maps](https://www.taylorfrancis.com/chapters/edit/10.1201/9780429054969-9/modular-tactical-influence-maps-dave-mark) **[T]**, chapter 9 of *Game AI Pro 360: Guide to Tactical AI* (publisher listing). **This is the single strongest primary attribution I obtained in Topic A** — author, chapter, and collection all consistent across two independent publisher pages.

- **[I]** Influence maps are the natural *shared substrate* for a many-unit TBS: one grid-wide field (threat, control, attrition risk) is computed once per turn and then read cheaply by every unit's utility function. This is the most cost-effective "many units" answer in the whole architecture list, because cost is amortized across all readers rather than paid per unit.

### A.2 Scale and performance (few units vs hundreds)

**What the evidence supports:**

- **[F]** MCTS is chosen for *large search spaces* and *fine-grained control* ([Técnico Lisboa dissertation](https://fenix.tecnico.ulisboa.pt/downloadFile/1689244997263072/94042-Manuel-Roxo_dissertacao.pdf)).
- **[T]** TBS search-space size is a named research problem, with node *grouping* studied as a remedy ([jstage](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/fss/35/0/35_382/_article/-char/en)).
- **[T]** A utility-AI library documents *performance* explicitly ([Trove performance.md](https://github.com/PhilSA/Trove/blob/saved/vo-old/com.trove.utilityai/Documentation%7E/performance.md)).
- **[F]** An HTN planner implementation compiles its methods to a host language *for performance* ([gtpyhop paper](http://www.cs.umd.edu/~nau/papers/nau2021gtpyhop.pdf)).
- **[T]** Asynchronous/streamed navigation processing is presented as the technique for *stable* pathfinding in games: [Asynchronous Navigation Processing: Stable AI Pathfinding in Games](https://www.havok.com/blog/asynchronous-navigation-processing/) — **this is the best available source for time-slicing/async work in the AI/nav pipeline.**
- **[T]** Reinforced by a commercial pathfinding package advertising volumetric/voxel A*: [PathFinderAI](https://marketplace.unity.com/packages/tools/behavior-ai/pathfinderai-volumetric-voxel-3d-2dastarnav-305575) **[T-weak]**.
- **[F]** Plan caching as a cost-reduction idea appears in the LLM-agent literature — *"Cost-Efficient Serving of LLM Agents via Test-Time Plan Caching"* ([ICML 2025](https://icml.cc/virtual/2025/51831), [arXiv HTML](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2506.14852)) **[F/T]**. **This is out-of-domain** (LLM agent serving, not game AI). I include it only as evidence that "cache the plan, reuse it" is a recognised general strategy — **do not present it as game-AI guidance.**

**Inferences [I]:**

1. **Two-tier architecture is the defensible reading of this evidence.** A cheap global tier (influence/threat fields computed once per turn) plus a per-unit tier (utility scoring over a small candidate action set) scales to hundreds of units because the expensive part is shared, not repeated.
2. **Expensive reasoning (MCTS, GOAP search, alpha-beta) must be budgeted and amortized**: only for a small "command" set of units, only for a few high-stakes decisions per turn, or over abstracted action groups.
3. **Hierarchical decomposition is the recurring answer to scale** — HTN decomposition, node grouping, and squad-level plans are three names for the same idea: decide coarsely, then refine locally.
4. **Time-slicing/caching should be *observable*, not implicit.** If plans carry a "computed at turn N / valid until precondition P" stamp, the engine can reuse them and also *serialize* them (see Topic D).
5. **Cached plans are a persistence hazard**: a cache whose invalidation is not part of the save format will produce save/reload divergence. This is my inference, but it is exactly the class of bug the AGENTS.md persistence rules target.

- **[I]** The defensible architecture is **one visibility computation, two readers**: the AI perceives through exactly the same visibility function the player does (so it can only act on `currently-visible` plus `last-known-contact` memory), and any deliberate cheating is an *explicit, named, configurable* parameter (e.g. `ai_sees_through_fog = false`, `ai_knows_starting_positions = true`) rather than an implicit side effect. The reason this matters technically, not just ethically: if the AI reads true state while the player reads the fogged view, then **AI decisions and replay/undo desynchronise from the player-visible state**, and any "AI is fair" claim becomes untestable.
- **[I]** AI fairness is therefore a *testability* requirement: to prove the AI doesn't cheat, the engine needs a switch that forces the AI to consume the player's fogged view, plus a way to assert the AI never read an unfogged cell.

### D.1 Full-state serialization vs command-log replay

- **Evidence [T] (state-capture approach, fragment-rich).** [gregorik/Rollback-Core](https://github.com/gregorik/Rollback-Core) **[F/T]** — described as *"Deterministic GGPO-style rollback netcode for Unreal Engine 5: fixed-step simulation, **SaveGame-reflection state capture**, UDP transport with input redundancy and reliable ACKs."* This is concrete evidence of the **snapshot** strategy: capture full simulation state via reflection, because a deterministic rollback needs to restore arbitrary states.

- **Evidence [F] (determinism is the enabling requirement).** *"Imagine the game of two players having the same state X"* — [Correlated randomness in Slay the Spire 2 — HN discussion](https://hn.nuxt.dev/item/48552844) **[F]**. And a fixed-point determinism library: [Parad0x84/DeterministicMath — "UE5 Deterministic math library (fixed point)"](https://github.com/Parad0x84/DeterministicMath) **[F/T]**, with an npm analogue [@shaisrc/fixed-point](https://socket.dev/npm/package/@shaisrc/fixed-point/files/0.1.0/README.md) **[T-weak]**. The existence of *fixed-point math libraries marketed specifically for determinism* is the strongest available evidence that **floating-point nondeterminism is a real, acknowledged blocker for replay/lockstep.**
- **Evidence.** [XCOM Expansion Grudgingly Acknowledges Save Scummers — RPS](https://www.rockpapershotgun.com/xcom-expansion-grudgingly-acknowledges-save-scummers) **[T]** — the *player-facing* consequence of cheap mid-battle save/load: save scumming becomes a balance problem the designers must answer. **This is the best available source for the design tradeoff of allowing mid-battle saves.**
- **[I] The tradeoff, stated as an engineering ledger (my synthesis, only the pieces above are sourced):**
  - **Full-state snapshot** — pros: load is O(state), works with arbitrary code (no need for a re-simulatable model), trivially supports *undo* and *mid-battle save*, and does not require bit-exact determinism. Cons: save size grows with world size; every new field is a compatibility event; it captures state you may not be able to *reconstruct* correctly (e.g. cached plans, RNG state, AI blackboards) unless you deliberately serialize them.
  - **Command-log replay** — pros: tiny saves (just the log + seed), self-documenting history, gives replays and undo almost for free, and makes desync detection possible. Cons: requires a *deterministic* simulation (fixed-point or strictly-controlled float, seeded RNG streams, stable iteration order), load time grows with log length, it breaks the moment any gameplay code changes semantics, and it is fragile against "the same input produced a different state" bugs. It also does not by itself restore *presentation* state.
  - **Hybrid** — snapshot + recent command log (the rollback pattern above) buys both cheap save and fast recovery, at the cost of doubling the correctness burden: you must keep *both* paths correct.
- **[U]** No source found that lays out this snapshot-vs-replay tradeoff as a *design article or GDC talk*. The claim "there is a GDC talk on save/load architecture" **was not verified** — see §D.3.

Also relevant is a checkpointing patent: [US20020160833A1](https://patentimages.storage.googleapis.com/af/d3/19/ef0c7d15b4d4a9/US20020160833A1.pdf) **[F]** with the fragment *"state as a precaution against hardware outages or other disruptions of the computer system's memory"*, and [US9268666](https://patentimages.storage.googleapis.com/4c/cd/78/de28613e5694fc/US9268666.pdf) **[F]** with *"writing to a memory location causes whatever information was previously at that memory location to be lost"*. **Patents are weak design sources** — cited only to show the concerns are long-standing.
- **Evidence [T].** Course-level treatment: [Lecture Notes — MMMO-4-Persistence (LMU Munich)](https://www.dbs.ifi.lmu.de/Lehre/mmmo/sose17/slides/MMMO-4-Persistence.pdf) **[T]**.
- **[I]** The requirements this evidence implies for a long-lived TBS (each element traceable to the sources above):
  1. A **schema id + monotonic version** written into the save, because migration is impossible without knowing the source version ([asgardtech/archer #662](https://github.com/asgardtech/archer/issues/662), [UE5 versioning post](https://www.strayspark.studio/blog/ue5-save-system-versioning-2026)).
  2. An **explicit unknown-field policy** (ignore / preserve / reject) — the general schema-evolution lesson from [tenant-shard-db](https://github.com/elloloop/tenant-shard-db/blob/main/docs/schema-evolution.md) and [Protobuf editions](https://protobuf.com.cn/editions/overview/) **[T]**.
  3. A **one-way migration chain** (`v1→v2→v3…`) rather than pairwise converters, so the number of migration paths stays linear.
  4. **Atomic writes** (temp + rename) so a crash mid-save cannot destroy the previous good save ([omlx commit](https://github.com/jundot/omlx/commit/a42d8375b3a18541a9abb87afd48d19205cf957d) **[T]**, [unicorn-game #64](https://github.com/cooneycw/unicorn-game/issues/64) **[T-weak]**).
  5. **No partial mutation on failed load** — validate/migrate into a staging structure, then commit. *This one is an inference, but it is also a literal rule in this repository's `AGENTS.md` ("Restore/import must not leave partially mutated observable state").*
  6. **Determinism contract** for anything that will be replayed: named seeded RNG streams and fixed-point or strictly controlled arithmetic ([DeterministicMath](https://github.com/Parad0x84/DeterministicMath), [Slay the Spire determinism discussion](https://hn.nuxt.dev/item/48552844)).
  7. **Explicitly serialize derived caches** (AI plans, influence fields, visibility bitsets) or guarantee they are rebuilt on load — otherwise the reloaded game behaves differently from the saved one. *Inference; no source found that states this for TBS AI specifically.*
  8. **Save-scumming is a design decision with balance consequences**, not merely a filesystem feature ([RPS on XCOM save scumming](https://www.rockpapershotgun.com/xcom-expansion-grudgingly-acknowledges-save-scummers) **[T]**). Mid-battle saves are cheap to add and expensive to balance.

## 5. Cross-cutting conclusion: what this research actually establishes

**The dominant, honest finding is a sourcing gap, not an answer gap.** For this exact topic quadrant, the open web index returns:

1. **Books and paywalled chapters** as the real authority (Taylor & Francis *Game AI Pro* chapters — the only *confirmed author attribution* in the whole exercise: Dave Mark, *Modular Tactical Influence Maps*; *An Introduction to Utility Theory*) **[T]**.
2. **Algorithm documentation with no game-specific guidance** (Red Blob Games, RogueBasin) **[T]**.
3. **A long tail of weak or self-published material** (forums, marketplace listings, AI-generated repos, product pages) that is *suggestive of what practitioners struggle with* but is **not** citable as architecture guidance.

**Concrete implication for the gap analysis:** where the engine needs decisions (AI architecture at scale, LOS symmetry, multi-level boards, cover semantics, AI fog fairness, save-vs-replay), the *decisions cannot be resolved by citation from this session*. They must be resolved by:
- reading the primary books/papers out-of-band (Orkin F.E.A.R.; Dave Mark's utility theory and influence-map chapters; the *Game AI Pro* volumes; the RogueBasin FOV articles; the redblobgames hex guide), and
- **writing decision records in-repo**, in the style of the two strongest engineering artefacts found — [opencombatengine ADR 0028 (grid system)](https://github.com/jamesplotts/opencombatengine/blob/33a3e316210227644595d0cf30f815c901c2f58f/docs/adr/0028-grid-system.md) and [ADR 0034 (movement cost)](https://github.com/jamesplotts/opencombatengine/blob/6cda6c35b82fb1682d39f61d442e8114e806f4fb/docs/adr/0034-movement-cost.md) **[T]**, which must be re-validated against real play, because their bodies were not read here.
